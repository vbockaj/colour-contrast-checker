About this tool

What it does

This tool checks whether a text and background colour pair is readable. It calculates the WCAG 2.2 contrast ratio and then shows how the same pair looks with protanopia, deuteranopia, tritanopia and in greyscale, recalculating the contrast for each view.

Why contrast alone is not enough

A good contrast ratio is necessary but not sufficient. A page can pass a contrast check and still lose information when colour is the only way something is marked, for example a link that differs from body text only by hue. The palette page flags colour pairs that pass in normal vision but drop below 3:1 under a simulation, because such pairs tend to merge for some users.

How it works

  • Contrast follows the WCAG relative luminance formula. AA needs 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text; AAA needs 7:1 and 4.5:1.
  • Simulations use the Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes (2009) matrices applied to linear RGB.
  • Greyscale represents monochromacy: what is distinguishable without colour is distinguishable in every other simulation.

Limitations

Simulations are approximations calculated by formula. They show where information may be lost, but they cannot replace testing with real users, because actual perception varies with the type and degree of colour-vision deficiency, lighting and experience. The severity slider is a simple blend towards the full simulation, not a clinical model.

Research background

The approach builds on a master's thesis on the accessibility of Croatian university websites for people with colour-vision deficiency, which combined automated WAVE checks with colour-blindness simulations and a seven-criterion evaluation matrix.
